# 9 ✦ Place & Direction

∞ ∞ ∞

Where something stands and which way it faces carries energy too: a building on its land, a desk in a room, a stand at a market, even the parts of a page. This page gathers the traditions of place. Building codes, safety and accessibility always win.

## Reading the Place

Before choosing a place, feel it and look at it: the light, the air, the sound, the water nearby, how people move through it. Geomancy, known in Europe and many other cultures, reads the land more deeply: where water runs beneath it, its earth energies (such as the Hartmann and Curry grids) and its places of strength or stress, often found by dowsing. Where people sleep, work and gather, choose calm, strong places.

## The Directions

Each element lives in a direction. Place colours, materials and shapes where their element lives.

| Direction | Feng Shui | Vastu Shastra |
|---|---|---|
| East | Wood | the rising sun: entrances, morning light |
| South-east | Wood and Fire | Fire: the kitchen |
| South | Fire | heavier, more closed walls |
| South-west | Earth | Earth: sleeping rooms, stability |
| West | Metal | rest, dining, study |
| North-west | Metal | Air: guests, movement |
| North | Water | wealth: open and light |
| North-east | Earth | Water: meditation, a fountain, the purest zone |
| Centre | Earth | Space (Ether): kept open |

The two traditions don't always agree. **Choose one tradition for a project and follow it throughout.**

**South of the equator,** some Feng Shui practitioners place Fire in the north, towards the noon sun, and Water in the south. Here too, choose one way and keep to it.

## Balance in a Room

A space with too much of one element feels it. A room of glass and steel is very Metal: add wood and plants. A dark, damp room is very Water: add warm light and earth tones. The cycles that balance the elements are on [Elements](7-elements.md#how-the-elements-relate-the-cycles).

## The Centre

All traditions of place honour the centre. Feng Shui gives it to Earth and health, Vastu keeps it open as the Brahmasthan, BioGeometry sees the centre as the source of balance. Keep the centre of a building, a room, a page or a logo calm and uncluttered.

## The Bagua

The Bagua map divides a space into nine areas, a 3 × 3 grid, each with its life theme, element and colour. It analyses rooms and buildings, and serves just as well as a guide for placing the parts of a logo, a page or a print. Laid with the entrance (or the reader's starting point) at the bottom:

|                                              |                                                   |                                            |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Wealth & abundance** · Wood · purple, gold | **Fame & reputation** · Fire · red                | **Love & relationships** · Earth · pink    |
| **Family & roots** · Wood · green            | **Health & centre** · Earth · yellow, earth tones | **Creativity & children** · Metal · white  |
| **Knowledge & wisdom** · Earth · blue        | **Career & path** · Water · black, deep blue      | **Helpful people & travel** · Metal · grey |

On a page or a flyer: the main message in the centre (health, balance), what the brand wants to be known for at the top (fame), the next step for the reader near the bottom (path).

In a shop, studio or home, lay the Bagua over the floor plan with the entrance at the bottom, and strengthen each area with its element and colour.

## The Vastu Mandala

Vastu lays a 9 × 9 grid over a plan, the Vastu Purusha Mandala: 81 squares, with the open centre (the Brahmasthan) in the middle. Each zone carries its direction and element (see the table above). Rooms are placed by their purpose: entrance in the east, kitchen in the south-east, sleeping rooms in the south-west, meditation in the north-east.
